# 33. Van der Waerden number W(4,4)

Family: Schur and van der Waerden numbers. Benchmark problem: `vdw-4-4`.

**Best known:** W(4,4) ≥ 1049, J. Rabung and M. Lotts (2012).

**To beat it:** a colouring of 1, ..., 1049 with 4 colours and no monochromatic 4-term arithmetic progression, which would show W(4,4) ≥ 1050.

**Keeper:** [Leaps in Bounds (D. Munro)](https://github.com/daniel-munro/leaps-in-bounds), read 2026-11-09 15:49 +04 (source data at commit a5bbf82 (9 Nov 2026, 05:15 UTC)).

## The problem

Colour n consecutive integers with 4 colours so that no colour holds an arithmetic progression of 4 terms, with n as large as possible; it shows W(4,4) > n. The best known lower bound is W(4,4) >= 1049, by Rabung and Lotts (2012), so such a colouring of 1048 integers is known and one of 1049 or more would improve it.

## Why it matters

W(4,4) asks for four colours and progressions of length four; its lower bound comes from Rabung and Lotts's cyclic zipper constructions. Van der Waerden's 1927 theorem is one of the founding results of Ramsey theory; the bound comes from cyclic colourings, and Monroe later ran the same method on every prime up to 950 million (arXiv:1603.03301), so a better colouring will likely need a different construction.

Sources: [Rabung and Lotts, Electron. J. Combin. 19(2) (2012)](https://doi.org/10.37236/2363); [Monroe, arXiv:1603.03301](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03301).
