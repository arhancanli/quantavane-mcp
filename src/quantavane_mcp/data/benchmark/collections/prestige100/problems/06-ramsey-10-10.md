# 06. Ramsey number R(10,10)

Family: Ramsey numbers. Benchmark problem: `ramsey-10-10`.

**Best known:** R(10,10) ≥ 798, R. Mathon (1987). Also known: R(10,10) ≤ 16064 (V. Angeltveit and B. D. McKay, 2019-2024).

**To beat it:** a red-blue colouring of the edges of K_798 with no red K_10 and no blue K_10, which would show R(10,10) ≥ 799.

## The problem

Find a two-colouring of the edges of K_n with no red K_10 and no blue K_10; it shows R(10,10) > n. The best known lower bound is R(10,10) >= 798, so a colouring of K_797 is known and one of K_798 or more would improve it.

## Why it matters

R(10,10) lies between 798 and 16064; the lower bound is Mathon's 1987 construction (DS1 Tables Ia and Ib). Diagonal Ramsey numbers are the classical form of Ramsey's theorem, and their lower bounds have resisted computer search for decades.

Sources: [Small Ramsey Numbers, rev. 18, Tables Ia and Ib](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/DS1).

Score: n, the number of vertices of the colouring; it proves R(10,10) >= n + 1 (larger is better).
