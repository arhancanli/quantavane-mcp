# 72. The (3,13)-cage

Family: Cages. Benchmark problem: `cage-3-13`.

**Best known:** n(3,13) ≤ 272, Hoare, reported by Biggs ([Bi89]). Also known: n(3,13) ≥ 202 (B. D. McKay, W. Myrvold and J. Nadon (1998)).

**To beat it:** a 3-regular graph of girth at least 13 on fewer than 272 vertices.

## The problem

Find a 3-regular simple graph whose girth (shortest cycle) is at least 13, with as few vertices n as possible; it shows n(3,13) <= n (cages grow with the girth). The best known (k,g)-graph in the House of Graphs cage table has 272 vertices [Bi89]; a graph with fewer vertices improves it.

## Why it matters

The smallest cubic graph of girth 13 is unknown: it has between 202 and 272 vertices, and the 272-vertex Cayley graph found by Hoare has stood since the 1980s (Dynamic Cage Survey). Royle showed no smaller Cayley graph exists, so a better graph must be of another kind.

Sources: [House of Graphs, cage table](https://houseofgraphs.org/meta-directory/cages); [Exoo and Jajcay, Dynamic Cage Survey, Electron. J. Combin. DS16 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.37236/37).
