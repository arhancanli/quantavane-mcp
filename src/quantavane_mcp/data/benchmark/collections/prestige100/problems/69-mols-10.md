# 69. Three mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order 10

Family: Projective planes and Latin squares. Benchmark problem: `mols-10`.

**Best known:** 2, E. T. Parker (1959). Also known: at most 8 (since 9 would give a projective plane of order 10).

**To beat it:** three Latin squares of order 10, each pair orthogonal.

**Keeper:** [The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](https://oeis.org), read 2026-11-09 16:14 +04 (A001231 (revision 35) and A001438 (revision 96)).

## The problem

Find as many mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order 10 as possible: Latin squares that, taken two at a time and superimposed, show each of the 100 ordered pairs of symbols exactly once. Two are known (Parker 1959; Bose, Shrikhande and Parker 1960); three would be new.

## Why it matters

Euler conjectured in 1782 that no two orthogonal Latin squares of order 10 exist; Parker found two in 1959, and whether three exist is open (OEIS A001438 notes the conjecture that two is the maximum). Order 10 is the smallest order where the maximum number of mutually orthogonal Latin squares is unknown.

Sources: [OEIS A001438](https://oeis.org/A001438); Bose, Shrikhande and Parker, Canad. J. Math. 12 (1960).
